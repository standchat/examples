How it works

Outdoor air holds more heat than you think

Even at −25 °C, air carries usable heat. Hollin Air gathers it, concentrates it and hands it to the water in your radiators, floors and hot-water tank. For every unit of electricity, you get about five units of heat.

  1. Gather

    A slow, quiet fan draws outdoor air across a coil filled with natural refrigerant, which boils even in deep frost.

  2. Lift

    A variable-speed compressor squeezes the vapour, raising its temperature to as much as 65 °C, using only as much power as the day needs.

  3. Warm

    The heat passes to the water in your heating circuit and hot-water tank. Most existing radiators work as they are.

FAQ

Questions people ask first

Does it really work in deep frost?

Yes. Hollin Air gives full heat down to −25 °C and keeps running below that, with a small built-in electric backup for the coldest nights.

Do I need new radiators?

Usually not. Water up to 65 °C suits most existing radiators. The survey checks yours.

What maintenance does it need?

No yearly service. The app runs a remote check-up once a year, and you keep the outdoor unit clear of snow and leaves.

Are there grants?

Many countries support switching from oil or gas. Your quote lists what applies to you, and we prepare the paperwork.

    How it works

    A lifelike host for a product page

    Ada is a real-time 3D person standing on the page, not a video in a box. She walks in when you arrive, listens while you type, thinks while the answer is written, then says it in a speech bubble: her lips follow the words, her hands land on the stressed ones, and when the answer points somewhere on the page she walks over and shows it. Your side of the conversation is a set of dialogue choices, as in an adventure game, plus room for your own words. Behind it all is Stand Chat, so an AI Stand-in answers, a person on the team can take over, and the history stays in Stand.

    Try it

    1. Reload and watch her walk in from the right, turn to you and wave. Leave her alone and she breathes, shifts her weight, glances at the page and your pointer, and now and then takes a few steps.
    2. Click her, or Talk to Ada, and pick a line: Give me the quick tour. Or type your own. She turns to you and leans in while you type, and looks away to think while the answer is written.
    3. Her answer appears in her speech bubble, a sentence at a time, in step with her mouth. When she mentions a part of the page, it scrolls into view and she walks over and presents it.
    4. Turn on her voice with the speaker button. Everything also works muted. The list button shows the whole conversation.
    5. Scroll down to this section and she steps off the page; scroll back up and she returns. On a phone she waits in the corner and stands on the dialogue bar while you talk.
    This page uses siteId: 'demo', Stand Chat's shared demo site, which works on any domain. Its demo Stand-in follows the page's private context. Hollin is made up, and Ada says so if you ask.

    The pieces

    • The character is Ada, an Epic MetaHuman exported by the open metahuman-to-glb pipeline with all 51 ARKit face shapes. Her license is in assets/LICENSES.md.
    • The Bitmagic GDK prepared and dresses her. Its MetaHuman tools merged her five skeleton copies into one and gave the clothes the skin's weights, and its runtime gives her skin subsurface light, hair and brows from their card atlases, wet eyes and corrective joints. The GDK installs from npm; bitmagic tools, which holds all of this, works without a Bitmagic Pro subscription.
    • performer.js is her acting: walking, posture, breath, weight shifts, arm and leg IK, gaze, blinks, expressions and gestures, all procedural.
    • speech.js turns a reply into a timeline of words, mouth shapes and stressed words, and plays the optional voice.
    • stage.js renders her with three.js (WebGPU, falling back to WebGL 2) on a transparent canvas just her size.
    • host.js places her on the page, keeps the bubble on her head, and runs the dialogue on Stand's Visitor API, through stand-client.js.

    1. Bring a MetaHuman to the web with the GDK

    The GDK ships its MetaHuman toolkit with the CLI. tools/prepare-ada.sh runs it on the upstream export, then finishes the file for a browser.

    npm install -g @bitmagic/cli
GDK=$(bitmagic tools path metahuman)/tools/metahuman

node $GDK/strip-draco.mjs ada.glb 1.glb             # GDK
node tools/adapt-ada.mjs 1.glb 2.glb                # 5.6 export → the GDK's naming
node $GDK/merge-skeletons.mjs 2.glb 3.glb           # GDK: five skeletons → one
node $GDK/reorder-body-first.mjs 3.glb 4.glb        # GDK
node $GDK/smooth-outfit-weights.mjs 4.glb 5.glb 12 0.6 Shirt
node $GDK/transfer-skin-weights.mjs 5.glb 6.glb     # GDK: no skin through the shirt
node tools/finish-ada.mjs 6.glb assets/ada.glb textures/   # bake, WebP, meshopt: 7.6 MB

    In the page, the GDK's MetaHumanActor adopts the loaded model and upgrades its materials. tools/build-gdk.mjs compiles the three toolkit modules it needs into vendor/bitmagic-metahuman.js, with Bitmagic's license notice.

    import { MetaHumanActor, VISEMES } from './vendor/bitmagic-metahuman.js';

const model = (await loader.loadAsync('assets/ada.glb')).scene;
const actor = new MetaHumanActor({ scene, loader }, {
  materials: { hairAtlasUrl: 'assets/hair.webp', browAtlasUrl: 'assets/brows.webp', lashCoverageUrl: 'assets/lashes.webp' },
  life: { blink: false, saccades: false, lookAtCamera: false }, // performer.js directs these
});
actor.adopt(model, model); // skin, hair cards, eyes, corrective joints
const performer = new Performer({ model, actor, visemes: VISEMES });

    2. Let the conversation drive the body

    Each state of the chat is a way of being. Typing in the box makes her lean in and nod now and then. While Stand reports an answer being written, she looks away to think and glances back. When the reply lands, she breathes in and speaks.

    client.subscribe((state) => {
  if (state.typing) performer.setMode('thinking');      // standin.status / typing events
  for (const m of newReplies(state.messages)) speak(m);  // a confirmed Stand-in message
});
input.addEventListener('input', () => performer.setMode('listening'));

function speak(message) {
  const timeline = buildTimeline(withoutMarkup(message.body), { refs });
  performer.say(timeline, clock);   // lips, beats, nods, glances, gestures
  bubble.follow(timeline, clock);   // the same clock lights up each word
}

    3. Turn words into a performance

    A reply becomes a timeline: each word gets a time and a string of mouth shapes, the stressed words get beats, nods and a lift of the brows, and the sentence ends get a nod or, for questions, a tilt. The mouth shapes are the GDK's viseme table on her ARKit blendshapes, blended into the next shape just before it starts, as real mouths do.

    buildTimeline('The Air 9 suits most homes…')
// → words: [{ text: 'Air', start: 0.31, end: 0.52, shapes: ['C', 'E'], stressed: true }, …]

planGestures(timeline)
// → [{ t: 0.12, type: 'look', where: 'away' }, { t: 0.13, type: 'gesture', name: 'beat' },
//    { t: 1.8, type: 'gesture', name: 'present' }, { t: 2.9, type: 'nod', size: 2 }, …]

    4. Walk over and point

    The private context lists the page's sections and shows one in use, so the Stand-in writes references like [[Savings]]. The bubble turns them into links, and as Ada reaches the word, the page scrolls to the section, she walks over beside the dialogue panel, turns to the content and presents it with an open hand.

    She lives on a transparent canvas just her size. Her feet stay at the canvas's origin while the floor moves under her, and the page moves the canvas by exactly as much, so her planted feet never slide across the page.

    performer.walkTo(spotInMetres, -0.45);   // walk there, then turn toward the page
stage.onFrame = () => {
  figure.style.transform = `translate3d(${performer.x * pxPerMetre - width / 2}px, ${top}px, 0)`;
  placeBubble(stage.landmarks().crown);  // the tail stays on her head
};

    5. What makes her feel alive

    • Nothing is ever still. She breathes about thirteen times a minute, faster when talking, and takes a breath before each sentence. Her weight moves from one hip to the other every few seconds.
    • Eyes lead. Her eyes jump to a new target first and her head follows a beat later on a spring. Micro-saccades keep the eyes busy while she holds your gaze, and a blink often covers a big glance.
    • Hands and feet by IK. Gestures are hand positions and palm turns, not joint angles. Two-bone IK finds the elbows and knees, and forearm and upper-arm twist is shared out to the twist bones, so wrists don't wring.
    • A walk is a pendulum. Each foot is planted until the step that lifts it; the hips ride as high as the planted legs allow, so they rise over the standing foot and dip when both are down, and sway toward it. Heels lift before the toes leave, the toes flex, the shoulders counter-rotate and the arms swing against the legs. She turns with small steps, and only gestures once both feet are down.
    • Talkers look away. People glance away as they plan a sentence and come back to you at the end of it. So does Ada.
    • The face does more than the mouth. Brows lift on emphasis, a smile softens while the mouth works, and listening, thinking and concern each have their own expression.

    Good to know

    • Say who's answering. Ada is a character, not the responder. The panel says Answers by an AI Stand-in, and if a person from the team takes over, their messages appear under their name and Ada doesn't voice them.
    • Out of the way. Clicks pass through to the page everywhere except Ada herself, her bubble and the panel. She stands in the margin and only walks over content while she's showing it.
    • Size. Ada is 7.6 MB, plus 1.7 MB of hair and lash atlases, and loads after the page. Phones and Data Saver load her only when you talk to her. Until then you see a picture.
    • Graceful everywhere. Without WebGPU she renders with WebGL 2. Without either, the picture stays and the chat still works. With reduce motion on, she doesn't walk or gesture with her arms, and the page doesn't scroll by itself.
    • Licenses. The example's own code is public domain. Ada is Epic's MetaHuman, under Epic's terms. The compiled GDK modules are Bitmagic's, under the PolyForm Shield license with a permission for interactive works. Both notices sit next to the files.

    If you try this yourself

    • Point the agent at the custom chat UI guide and the GDK's MetaHuman tools (bitmagic tools path metahuman).
    • Ask for motion built from posture, breath, gaze and IK, not joint angles on sine waves.
    • Make the agent look at its own work: filmstrips of the character speaking catch what code review can't.
