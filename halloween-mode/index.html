The night shift

Six regulars, five ratings, one costume

Each one has its own entrance, exit and opening line, and its look follows the rating. They use your page, too: the cat climbs onto these cards, the pumpkin peeks over them, and cobwebs grow in their corners.

  • The host

    The ghost

    Comes out when nobody has moved for a while, says hi, and goes home when you’re busy. Gentle ratings float out of the chat button; scarier ones rise from behind your page, or are just there.

  • Night shift

    Swoop across in ones, twos and threes, or as a swarm in NC-17. Now and then one stops to hang upside down from the top of the window or under a header.

  • Hangs around

    Drops in on a thread from the top of the window, or from under a heading or a card, dangles a while, and climbs back up. Scroll, and it bounces.

  • Head of lighting

    Jack

    Rises from the bottom of the window, or peeks up from behind a card or an image, and flickers like a real candle. Brighter when you come close.

  • Very curious

    Midnight

    Makes itself at home: walks in, leaps from card to card, sits a while and watches your pointer. Rest the pointer on a card and it comes over.

  • Rare guest

    The witch

    Crosses the top of the window now and then. A broom on its own in G, the witch herself in PG and PG-13, a raven in R and a murder of crows in NC-17.

Plus a costume for your chat button. Stand’s launcher wears a witch’s hat for the season (horns in NC-17), and takes it off when the chat opens.

How it works

A costume for the chat you already have

Halloween mode doesn’t replace Stand’s widget. It draws a few characters on a layer above your page, lets them sit on and hang from what’s already there, and when one is clicked, it opens Stand’s own chat with that character’s greeting, through Stand’s public JavaScript API.

1. Paste it into any page

Nothing to install. These two lines work on any page, even a local HTML file: Stand Chat with its shared demo site, which answers anywhere, and Halloween mode straight from this site, characters included.

<script defer src="https://cdn.stand.chat/widget/stand.js" data-stand-id="demo"></script>
<script defer src="https://examples.stand.chat/halloween-mode/halloween.js" data-rating="pg"></script>

It runs from October 1 to November 1 every year and stays in its box the rest of the time. Add ?halloween=on to the page’s address to see it any day, ?halloween=r to try another rating, or ?halloween=off to see the page without it.

2. Answer with your own Stand

Swap demo for your Site ID from Sites in Stand. Now your own Stand-ins and team answer, and every chat starts with the character’s line and a private note about how the visitor got there.

<script defer src="https://cdn.stand.chat/widget/stand.js" data-stand-id="YOUR-SITE-ID"></script>

3. Pick a rating

Change data-rating and the whole cast changes with it: same choreography, five temperaments. The rating sets the art, the lines, the speech bubbles, what bursts out on a click, and the tone the private prompt asks of whoever answers.

Let them use your page

Characters look at what’s on screen: cards, images and buttons with a visible edge, headings, and bars. The cat walks along top edges and leaps between them, the pumpkin and the ghost peek over them, spiders and bats hang from bottom edges, and cobwebs grow in corners. Everything follows the page as it scrolls, and nothing settles on links, buttons or fields you might click. Mark elements to steer them:

<div class="product-card" data-halloween="perch web">…</div>  <!-- sit on it, web its corner -->
<h2 data-halloween="hang">Opening hours</h2>              <!-- spiders and bats hang here -->
<img src="hero.jpg" alt="" data-halloween="peek">           <!-- the ghost and pumpkin peek over -->
<form data-halloween="none">…</form>                     <!-- keep everything off -->

With data-perch="marked", only marked elements are used. With data-perch="off", everyone stays on the window’s edges.

Choose who comes, and how often

Options are data-* attributes on the same tag. Leave one out to keep its default.

<script defer src="https://examples.stand.chat/halloween-mode/halloween.js"
  data-rating="pg"
  data-cast="ghost bats pumpkin cat"
  data-haunt="calm"
  data-ghost-greeting="Boo! Looking for our opening hours? I can help."></script>

Drive it from JavaScript

Set window.StandHalloweenConfig before the script for what doesn’t fit in an attribute, like several lines per character or labels in your language. window.StandHalloween takes it from there.

window.StandHalloweenConfig = {
  rating: 'pg',
  greetings: {
    ghost: ['Boo! Looking for our opening hours?', 'Still here? Ask me anything.'],
    pumpkin: 'Lit to see you! The pumpkin spice menu is back.',
  },
  context: 'Kaisla is a public lakeside sauna, open 7–22 every day.',
  labels: { ask: 'Kysy meiltä', hint: 'Avaa chat', dismiss: 'Ei nyt' },
};

StandHalloween.configure({ rating: 'r' }); // Everyone goes home; the R cast arrives.
StandHalloween.summon('cat');              // Someone drops by now.
StandHalloween.stop();                     // Everyone goes home.

document.addEventListener('stand-halloween-open', (event) => {
  analytics.track('Halloween chat', { creature: event.detail.creature, rating: event.detail.rating });
});

StandHalloween.art('pumpkin', 'r') returns any character in any rating as an element that fits itself into the box you put it in, for your 404 page or a banner.

How a character opens the chat

Halloween mode never talks to Stand’s servers itself. It waits until Stand has someone who can answer, and every click goes through StandChat.openChat. The character’s line becomes the greeting of a new conversation, a private prompt tells whoever answers how the visitor got there and which tone to strike, and analyticsId shows you in Stand which character brought the chat in.

StandChat.whenAvailable(() => start()); // Only once someone can answer.

// What a click on the R-rated tarantula sends:
StandChat.openChat('Eight eyes, all on you. What do you need?', {
  prompt: 'Halloween mode is on for this website… The visitor opened this chat by '
    + 'clicking a hairy tarantula… Open your first reply with one deadpan, creepy '
    + 'line in its spirit… Creepy fun only: no gore, no threats. Then help…',
  analyticsId: 'halloween-spider',
});

When a conversation is already open, openChat just brings it back, so a second character never interrupts. While the chat is open, the characters step back, and they return a little after it closes.

Host it yourself

The copy on this site changes when this example does. To keep yours exactly as it is, download halloween.js and the image for your rating into a folder named halloween next to it: g, pg, pg-13, r or nc-17, 150 to 210 KB each. Only the rating on show is ever downloaded. If the images live somewhere else, data-assets points the script at them.

Things to try on this page

  • Keep still for a few seconds. The ghost comes out: from the chat button in G and PG, from behind something on the page in PG-13 and R, and out of nowhere in NC-17.
  • Click a character or the ghost’s bubble. The demo Stand-in answers in the character’s tone, then helps.
  • Scroll down to the cast. The cat leaps from card to card, the pumpkin peeks over them, and cobwebs grow in their corners. Rest your pointer on a card and the cat comes over.
  • Type b o o anywhere on the page.

Good to know

  • Polite by default. One or two visitors at a time. They don’t settle on links, buttons or fields, and nobody new arrives while someone types in a form or chats. While the ghost talks, Stand’s own greeting bubble steps aside, so there’s one greeting at a time.
  • The ghost knows when to go. It waits a moment when the visitor comes back, then goes home. The × or Esc sends it home for half an hour and calms everyone else down.
  • Reduced motion is respected. With prefers-reduced-motion, nobody flies, walks or jumps. A few characters just appear and sit still, and there’s no mist or grain.
  • It stays out of your styles. Everything lives in a shadow root just under Stand’s widget. The layer lets clicks through everywhere except on the characters, and the speech bubbles borrow your page’s font, except where a rating has its own.
  • One script, one image. The script is about 40 KB gzipped, and your rating’s image 150 to 210 KB, downloaded once and cached. No cookies. The animation loop runs only while someone moves, and sleeps in background tabs.
  • The costume is best-effort. It looks for Stand’s chat button, and isn’t worn when the button is hidden. data-costume="none" skips it.
  • The characters are extra doors. They’re hidden from screen readers and skipped by the keyboard, so Stand’s own button stays the accessible way into the chat.

Change anything

Halloween mode is public domain, art included: copy it, change it, ship it, no credit needed. Each rating is one image plus a table in the script of where its parts are, so you can swap in your own characters too. The quickest way to make it yours is to copy the folder and tell an AI coding agent what you want. For example:

In halloween.js, give the cat a favorite spot. On pages with an element marked data-halloween="cat-bed", it heads there first, hopping from card to card, curls up, and stays until the visitor scrolls past. Clicked there, it opens the chat with “You found my bed. What can I find for you?”

How this was made

Claude Opus 5.5, with max effort, in Claude Code, built this in one session from the prompt below and a run of follow-ups. Before writing any code, it read the coverage of Rivian’s 2026 Halloween update to find the voice: playful, warm and never actually scary, with named characters and a pun when one fits. It read stand.js to learn how to dress the chat button and notice when the chat opens, and checked the motion with filmstrips from headless Chrome.

Its first cast was drawn in SVG, and SVG couldn’t do horror. So the image model in Codex CLI drew ten characters for each rating, and then redrew each one as a sheet of puppet parts: a body, wings, legs, a tail, a lantern. Claude matched every part back onto the whole character in headless Chrome, packed each rating into one image, and kept the animation code it had written for the SVG cast.

The cat became the star. It has a skeleton of its own (a pelvis and a chest, a head, legs in three pieces and a tail in five), posed every frame with inverse kinematics so its paws stay planted while it walks, crouches, wiggles, leaps from card to card and lands. Bats beat jointed wings that fold at the wrist. Ravens and crows flap in three drawn poses, because a wing beating toward you can’t be faked by turning a flat picture.

A seasonal layer like this doesn’t need a custom chat UI. Stand’s widget stays as it is, and openChat carries a greeting and a private prompt for each character.

The prompt, lightly edited

Create a new example: Halloween mode. In this mode, Halloween-themed items animate onto and off the page. Interacting with them opens a chat. If there is no interaction, a friendly ghost wanders on the page as a greeting for the chat. Be creative. Make this easy to plug into any page that uses Stand Chat by including just one JS file and its assets, and make it configurable. Include instructions on how to use it on the example’s page. This was inspired by Rivian’s Halloween mode. When pondering the tone of voice, check Rivian’s 2026 Halloween mode update. One-shot this to very high quality: something people might be inspired to include on their own pages. The og image should demonstrate what it is, and also be enough of an advertisement for “Stand Chat Halloween mode”.

The follow-ups

The basic idea is good. Visually this seems pre-school themed, which might be a bad fit for some websites. Could we provide five versions named after the movie rating system, and a slider on the page for switching between them? Can the decorations recognize page elements and interact with them, or be placed using them? The G to NC-17 progression isn’t working well. Compare it with what Codex generated. The functionality is good, but the graphics go from bad (G) to horrific in the wrong way (NC-17). Generated SVG doesn’t cut it for horror. You can use an image generation model with Codex CLI. My intuition: generate the characters with it, slice them into parts, and animate them as you do now, but with pixmaps. The cat animation needs more work. Maybe cats need more joints. Put effort into it: the cat could be the heart of the whole experience, not just walking but jumping to page elements in a cat-like manner. The crow animation isn’t convincing either: the wings need more joints. Fix the bats too. Make the onboarding simpler: a first step you can paste into any page without installing anything, with the demo site ID and the script served from this site. End with a reminder that it’s free to modify, and an example AI prompt.

A few smaller notes followed, on the hero’s copy, a working snippet in its box, sound on by default, and a rating slider in the snippet builder.

If you try this yourself

  • Point the agent at a voice you like, here Rivian’s Halloween update, and let it research before it writes a line of copy.
  • When your idea decorates the page rather than replacing the chat, ask for a drop-in script on Stand’s JavaScript API. For a chat that looks completely different, start from the custom chat UI guide.
  • Start on the demo Site ID, so the agent can open real chats while it builds, and ask it to test the replies. Here, the private prompt went through three rewrites before the Stand-in opened with “I’m just here for the boos, not the business hours.”
  • Ask it to check its own work in a browser. Filmstrips caught a pumpkin and a cat fighting over the same corner, and a dimming effect that looked like dirt on light pages.
  • For animated characters, let an image model draw and the agent rig. Ask for each character whole, in a pose that’s easy to cut (wings spread, legs apart, the lantern hanging free), then for the same character as a sheet of puppet parts.
